# Design study checklist

A reading list for the design process. Work through a topic before writing a class C or D design doc that leans on it. Tick a topic when you can explain it in your own words and point to where this game does or does not use it.

## Topics

- [ ] **Core pillars.** The few things the game is about. Every change serves one (see section 8 of `docs/CHANGE_PIPELINE.md`).
- [ ] **The five-step development process.** Idea, design, build, test, release and review, as laid out in the pipeline gates.
- [ ] **Mechanical analysis.** Break a mechanic into inputs, outputs, costs and loops; find what it rewards.
- [ ] **Player psychology.** What players feel and do when a number moves, a timer runs or a reward lands.
- [ ] **Constructive iteration.** Change one thing, measure, keep or revert; critique that proposes a fix.
- [ ] **Probability and RNG.** Variance, streaks, and whether chance can be farmed.
- [ ] **Progression curves.** Fast and cheap early, slow and expensive late. Check the curve numbers from the balance harness.
- [ ] **Economy modeling.** Sources, sinks and loops, anchored to the MI-hour.
- [ ] **System deconstruction.** Take a well-known game system apart to see why it works.
- [ ] **Target audience.** Who plays, how often, and what they want from a session.

## Notes for a nation simulator

- Signposting and feature framing (guiding attention, presenting features) matter less for nation simulators than for other genres; spend study time elsewhere first.
- Progression attracts players; substance keeps them. When growth slows, players need something else to get better at and do (conflict, diplomacy, projects, rivalry). See section 4c of the design doc template.
- Waiting should never be the only option. Check every timer against the dead-time audit.

## Use

Cite the topics you used in the design doc's alternatives or system-map section. If a design ignores one that applies, say why.
